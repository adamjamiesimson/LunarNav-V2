Science & methods

Ephemeris validation

We compared 90 independently queried Sun and Earth angles from NASA JPL Horizons with LunarNav’s analytical model. The sample covers 45 site and UTC epochs across three sites and three seasons.

Visibility and mission windows

Local azimuth and elevation come from a truncated analytical ephemeris. A body counts as visible when its center is above the applied horizon. The model omits disc radius, refraction, eclipses and spacecraft shadows.

Earth visibility uses line-of-sight geometry. A radio link budget is required to assess communications. Site and date comparisons sample every 2 hours. The mission-window search samples every 4 hours, so results can differ. The balanced ranking weights simultaneous access at 70%, sunlight at 15% and Earth visibility at 15%.

Open mission planning

Power and storage

The model uses a fixed horizontal solar array, 1361 W/m² solar irradiance, constant electrical load and a fully charged battery at mission start. Battery charge is clamped to capacity. Battery chemistry, thermal behavior and radio link budgets are outside the model.

Open engineering

Terrain and surface views

The globe uses NASA LROC imagery with illustrative lighting. The Sun and Earth are shown at modeled directions with compressed sizes and distances. Globe relief exaggeration affects the display only. NASA Moon imagery.

Bundled relief uses an 8192 × 4096 global grid (~1.33 km at the equator) and 480 m polar grids. Custom points use these grids for a coarse horizon. Native refinement uses 60–80 m LOLA GeoTIFFs south of 60°S and ~237 m SLDEM2015 between 60°S and 60°N, subject to external range access. The north cap uses the bundled 480 m grid.

Horizon profiles sample to 20 km with lunar curvature and radial steps at DEM resolution. Runtime profiles use 1° azimuth bins and archived profiles use 5°. Small hazards, narrow occlusions and terrain beyond the sample radius may be missed. Imported GeoTIFF provenance is unverified. Sites without terrain use a flat 0° horizon.

The surface panorama uses NASA LOLA and Kaguya elevations and the modeled Sun direction. Surface texture and stars are illustrative. The Sun (0.53°) and Earth (1.98°) appear at their approximate angular sizes.

Traverse and transit estimates

Traverse distance follows a great circle on a spherical Moon. Visibility can use the terrain horizon at the origin. Slope, obstacles, route terrain and rover traversability are outside the estimate.

Earth-to-Moon schedules use reference transfer durations. Arrival, orbit insertion, descent and touchdown are distinct estimated events. Destination coordinates identify the landing site. The model does not solve trajectories, calculate delta-v or find launch windows.

Scope of use

LunarNav supports early mission screening. Flight operations require independent assessment of landing safety, power, communications and flight readiness. Illustrative points lack site surveys. LunarNav is an independent prototype without NASA affiliation or endorsement.
