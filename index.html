Methods and limitations

Select a site or click the globe, set a UTC date and duration, then inspect the timeline. Drag the globe to rotate; use Focus to centre the site. Plan Mission compares sites and dates; Engineering uses the same selected mission period.

Independent validation

The analytical Sun/Earth geometry was compared with NASA JPL Horizons results for three lunar sites across three seasonal windows. Maximum sampled errors were approximately 0.480° azimuth and 0.285° elevation. These are sample-specific results, not universal precision guarantees.

Open engineering

Model assumptions

  • Solar array: fixed and horizontal, no tracking; sunlight constant at 1361 W/m².
  • Electrical load is constant and the battery is idealised (no chemistry, no thermal behaviour, charge clamped to capacity).
  • No RF link budget: “geometric Earth visibility” is line-of-sight geometry only — not confirmed communication availability.
  • Visibility counts a body's center above the horizon; disc radius, refraction, eclipses and spacecraft shadows are not modelled.
  • Horizon precision is limited by terrain sampling (1° azimuth bins, DEM-resolution radial steps): very narrow occlusions can still be missed.
  • Ephemeris is a truncated analytical series (±~0.5° verified sample errors), not JPL/SPICE.

Terrain data (NASA LOLA)

Bundled relief covers the whole Moon: 8192 × 4096 globally (~1.33 km at the equator), plus 480 m north and south polar grids. Custom points derive a coarse horizon from these grids. Native upgrades use 60–80 m LOLA GeoTIFFs in 60°S–90°S and ~237 m SLDEM2015 rows between 60°S and 60°N, subject to external range access. The north cap currently has bundled 480 m terrain only. Each result reports its actual source and resolution.

Horizons cover 20 km, with lunar curvature and native pixel radial sampling; runtime profiles use 1° bins, archived site profiles 5°. Far-field terrain and small hazards are unresolved. Imported GeoTIFF provenance is unverified. Displacement exaggeration changes the view only. Surface-view camera calculations exist; a surface renderer is not implemented.
